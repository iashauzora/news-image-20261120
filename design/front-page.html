LEAD · MARKETS

Asian Markets Plunge
on AI-Bubble Fears

Taiwan sheds 2,200 points in a day as a regional sell-off overshadows Anthropic's record IPO

Asian stock markets suffered steep losses on Friday as fears of an AI bubble drove a regional sell-off. Taiwan's weighted index plunged more than 2,200 points intraday, breaking below 37,000 to its lowest level since mid-April — a fall of more than 7,000 points in a single week that pushed the island's regulator to unveil five rescue measures.

The rout came despite the week's landmark listing: Anthropic, the first frontier AI company to go public, began index inclusion after its record IPO. Wall Street offered no shelter — the S&P 500, Nasdaq and Dow all ended lower as the debut failed to restore confidence.

INDIA · WEATHER

Cyclone Akvan Makes Landfall

Cyclone Akvan crossed India's east coast near Gopalpur, Odisha, on Friday morning with winds of 70–80 km/h gusting to 90 — the country's third cyclone landfall in less than a month. Odisha evacuated over 300,000 people; no major damage was reported.

CLIMATE · COP31

Africa Seeks Tripled Climate Finance

As the UN climate summit entered its final day, African negotiators pressed to triple climate finance while European delegates urged a focus on existing pledges. Nations were still seeking deeper fossil-fuel cuts as the talks ran to the wire.

MIDDLE EAST

Houthi Strikes Damage Riyadh Landmark

Houthi forces claimed drone and missile strikes on four targets in Saudi Arabia. Saudi authorities confirmed an attack damaged the kingdom's tallest skyscraper in Riyadh, while Saudi air defences destroyed two incoming drones.

PACIFIC · WEATHER

Xavier Lashes the Northern Cooks

Severe Tropical Cyclone Xavier held Category 4 strength on Friday as RSMC Nadi kept a gale warning on Suwarrow and a storm alert on Nassau and Pukapuka. The storm, which damaged homes in the Northern Cooks, is weakening as it moves southeast.

GAMES · BUSINESS

GTA VI's First Day Tops $2.36 Billion

Estimates reported Friday put Grand Theft Auto VI's launch-day sales at about 24.6 million copies worth $2.36 billion — more than double GTA V's first-day record. The Sensor Tower figures are estimates; Take-Two's official numbers are still to come. Reviews average a near-perfect 99.
